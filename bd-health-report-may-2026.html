Executive Summary

Across 7 months, Rachel carried 229 of 319 assigned leads (72%) — averaging 7.6 leads per week across PE and OQ — making her the primary inbound handler by a significant margin. Rachel’s estimated weekly workload sits at 18.2 hours on average, which is within her contracted 20 hours on a normal week. However, 15 out of 29 weeks exceeded her net capacity, with peak weeks reaching an estimated 38 hours. Her PE conversion has collapsed from 15–20% in Nov–Dec 2025 to 0–9.5% from Jan 2026 onwards, and her OQ completion has followed the same downward trajectory.

On Rachel’s request for a base increment: the average week does not justify an increment — she is broadly being paid for what she does. The peaks are real but the output during those peaks does not reflect proportional effort. 117 leads ended with no response. A commission scheme would be far more attractive if her conversion rate were healthy — the preference for base over commission is itself a signal. The data does not support an increment.

On a full-time BD Executive at RM4,000: Camen handled only 45 leads at 1.5/week and converted at 53.5% — nearly double Rachel’s rate — demonstrating that focused ownership with adequate time produces better outcomes. A full-timer with the right background delivers double the hours at RM23.10/hr versus Rachel’s RM34.50/hr, absorbs all peak weeks, and has the bandwidth for full follow-through across inbound and outbound. At a projected 20% PE conversion the revenue uplift is estimated at ~RM12,600/month against a RM1,000/month salary difference. The ROI case is clear — but the workflow and triage system should be defined before the hire so the new person steps into a structured role from day one.

Findings & Team Recommendations
01
Rachel’s OQ Completion Has Collapsed Since Jan 2026
Rachel hit 100% OQ completion in Nov 2025 and 85.7% in Dec. From January onwards it dropped to 33%, 25%, 7% in Apr, and 10% in May. This isn’t just a PE problem — it has now spread into her Offline Queries pipeline too. Volume stayed roughly consistent (~10–16 OQ leads/month) but completion has cratered. As her unresolved PE backlog grew, it consumed the follow-up time that was previously available for OQ closings. The two queues are actively competing with each other and the OQ pipeline is losing.
02
117 No-Response Leads Represent Real Lost Revenue
117 of Rachel’s assigned leads ended with zero follow-through. At the venue’s typical private event value, even recovering 15–20% of those with a proper second-touch system would materially change the revenue picture. This isn’t a conversion rate problem on the leads that got worked — it’s a throughput problem on leads that never got a fair chance. An automated 48h and 96h follow-up nudge on all unanswered PE enquiries should be the first operational fix, before any resourcing decision is made.
03
Camen Is Under-Utilised Given Her Stronger Conversion Rate
Camen’s OQ completion rate is 53.5% — nearly double Rachel’s all-channel rate of 22.7%. Yet she was assigned only 2 PE enquiries across 7 months and handled a fraction of the total volume. The February 2026 anomaly (0/6 completions, all No Response) is worth investigating separately but does not change the overall picture. Camen should be handling a larger share of high-intent PE enquiries and OQ private event leads. Her capacity is being left on the table.
04
20 Unassigned Leads Represent a Process Failure, Not a People Failure
20 leads across the period had no assignee and were never worked. This points to a gap in the intake process — no one is owning the queue assignment step. A simple rule fixes this completely: any lead unassigned within 24 hours auto-escalates to the team lead. This costs nothing to implement and closes a real revenue leak.
05
Nov–Dec 2025 Proves the Baseline Works When Capacity Is Right
Both Rachel and Camen hit 100% OQ completion in November 2025. Rachel’s PE conversion was 15–20% in the first two months. This is not a hiring mistake — the role worked well when volume was manageable. The deterioration is entirely traceable to load accumulation from January onwards. That context matters when deciding whether the fix is people or process.
06
Triage by Intent Level Is the Highest-Leverage Workflow Change Available
The PE form already tags every lead: “Just exploring options”, “Interested and considering it”, or “Very interested and ready to discuss further”. Currently all three tiers get identical manual treatment, which means the person most likely to convert is waiting in the same queue as someone who clicked a form out of curiosity. Auto-reply for explorers, standard response for considering, and same-day priority follow-up for very interested — this single change would likely recover PE conversion to 18–22% without any additional headcount.

A
On the Increment Request — The Data Partially Supports Her, But Not the Ask
The average week sits at 19.8 estimated hours — essentially exactly her contracted 20 hours. On a “normal” week, Rachel is not overworked. However, 15 out of 29 weeks exceeded her net lead-handling capacity, including five weeks that likely required 28–38 hours of genuine work. Those peaks are real and compounding — unresolved leads from a heavy week spill into the next week and pile up.

The problem with awarding a base increment based on this: the output during those peak weeks is not proportional to the input. W11 had 19 leads and resulted in the worst monthly conversion in the dataset (Jan 2026: 0%). If Rachel were putting in 38 hours that week, the conversion should have held up, not collapsed. The hours claim and the output don’t reconcile. This suggests the response to overload was to handle volume at surface level — log leads, send first responses — and let follow-through go.

Recommended position: Acknowledge the peaks are real and propose a lead cap (max 12 PE leads/week) as an operational fix. Tie any base increment discussion to a 60-day performance window with a minimum 18% PE conversion target. A base increase without a conversion condition rewards the wrong behaviour.
B
On the Commission Preference — Commission Is Unattractive Because Conversion Is Low, Not the Other Way Around
Rachel’s stated reason for preferring base over commission is that “the commission scheme is not attractive enough.” It is worth noting that commission schemes become unattractive when conversion is low. At her current 9.3% PE conversion rate, commission income would be minimal. At 20–25% conversion — which the Nov–Dec 2025 data shows is achievable — commission would be meaningfully higher than any incremental base increase.

The preference for guaranteed base over performance-linked commission is a signal worth noting when assessing long-term fit for a BD role. A BD person who is converting well will always prefer commission. One who isn’t will always prefer base.

C
The Case for a Full-Time BD Executive Is Clear — But the Role Needs to Be Set Up Properly First
The data makes a strong argument for a full-time hire: double the hours at 33% lower cost-per-hour, peak week coverage, and the ability to own the pipeline end-to-end including on-ground presence at events and activations. At 20% PE conversion on current monthly volume (~30 leads/month), a full-timer would close approximately 6 PE events/month versus Rachel’s current 1.8. At an average event value of RM3,000, that’s a ~RM12,600/month revenue difference against a RM1,000/month salary gap.

The right profile is someone with a BD or community background, ideally from lifestyle, hospitality, or venue sectors — someone comfortable both managing inbound enquiries and doing outbound prospecting, corporate outreach, and on-ground engagement. This is a meaningfully broader role than what Rachel was hired to do.

The one caveat: the structural gaps that contributed to the current performance issues — no lead triage, no weekly cap, no escalation rules, no pipeline review cadence — need to be fixed before or alongside any new hire. Putting the right person into a broken system will produce the same results at a higher cost. Define the workflow first, then hire into it.
