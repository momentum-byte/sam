GOL · Seed Phase · Field Research Protocol

Before we build the solution,
we validate the problem.

A six-week ground canvassing sprint across three PPR communities in the Klang Valley — run by a student working group, designed to prove or kill GOL's core assumptions before a single ringgit is raised.

Field windowJuly – August 2026
Working group3–4 students + founder
Sites3 PPR, Klang Valley
DecisionGo / Pivot / Kill by early Sept
01 · Why this exists

The missing SEED phase

Advisor feedback on the GOL proposal was clear: it is solution-focused, and the problem statement has not been validated. The scope of beneficiaries is imprecise, the sport-to-digital retention link is assumed rather than tested, and the plan jumped to PILOT and LAUNCH with no SEED phase in front of it.

This canvassing sprint is that SEED phase. The goalpost is not to confirm GOL — it is to search for reasons GOL should not exist, and only proceed if it survives. Empathize → Explore → Execute, in that order. Fail fast, fail cheap, before institutional money is involved.

02 · What we are testing

Three hypotheses, one riskiest assumption

Written in the standard form: we believe [capability] will result in [outcome], and we will know we have succeeded when [measurable signal].

H1 · The problem is real

We believe youth aged 13–21 in PPR communities lack safe, structured pathways from free time to skills and opportunity, and this gap is felt — not imagined by us.

SIGNAL≥60% of youth and parents describe this gap unprompted, in their own words, before GOL is ever mentioned.
H2 · Sport is a credible hook

We believe regular sport participation can retain PPR youth into digital-skills programming — the desirability link the advisor flagged as unproven.

SIGNALIn week 5's smoke test, a pre-set share of engaged youth voluntarily signs up for a paired sport + digital taster session.
H3 · The gap is unserved

We believe existing programmes — school, surau, KBS, other NGOs — are not adequately meeting this need. If the competitive scan shows they are, GOL duplicates rather than adds.

SIGNALMapping of every programme that has touched each site in 3 years: what it did, why it ended, what residents say about it.
Riskiest assumption — test first

The sport → digital skills transfer. Everything in GOL's model depends on it, and it is currently supported by zero primary evidence. If youth come for the court but won't stay for the classroom, GOL as designed does not work. This is the experiment.

03 · Who does the work

The working group

Role 1

Field Lead

Coordinates site visits, holds the JPKK relationship, owns the schedule and safety protocol. Reports to founder weekly.

Roles 2–3

Field Interviewers ×2

BM-fluent, run intercepts and sit-down interviews in pairs, capture verbatim notes. Never interview a minor unaccompanied.

Role 4

Data & Synthesis

Transcribes, codes findings against the three hypotheses weekly, maintains the evidence log that becomes the final report.

Recruitment channels: university sports science and social science cohorts, plus youth volunteer pipelines via Impact Integrated. Half-day training covers non-leading interview technique, consent and child-safeguarding protocol (parental consent for all under-18 interviews), and data handling. Each student receives a modest honorarium plus transport — enough to signal the work is professional, not enough to distort motivation.

04 · Where

Three sites, chosen for contrast

Sites are selected against criteria — not convenience — and confirmed through warm introductions (community leaders in the GOL network, Impact Integrated, JPKK) before week 0 ends.

Criterion A

Density contrast

One high-density inner-KL PPR, one Selangor-edge PPR, one mid-scale site — so findings aren't an artefact of a single community type.

Criterion B

Court condition spread

At least one active court and one neglected court. If the problem only exists where courts are dead, that reshapes the model.

Criterion C

Gatekeeper access

A named JPKK or community champion willing to open doors — with a plan to interview beyond who the gatekeeper hand-picks.

05 · How — six weeks

The sprint

Week 0Setup

Secure access letters and JPKK introductions at all three sites. Recruit and train students. Finalise discussion guides. Founder walks each site once.

Weeks 1–2Observe & immerse

Court usage mapping: who plays, when, ages, gender split, organised vs ad-hoc. Community walkabouts and casual intercepts. No GOL pitch — listening only.

Gate 1End of week 2

Access confirmed at all 3 sites, ≥20 observation hours logged per site, usage maps drafted. If access has failed anywhere, swap the site now.

PASS / SWAP SITE

Weeks 3–4Generative interviews

Per site: structured conversations across five respondent groups, with focus groups where natural clusters form. Target floor: 60 conversations across the three sites.

Week 5Comprehension + smoke test

Read the GOL value proposition and tagline aloud in BM; ask respondents to repeat it back in their own words (comprehension test). Then the smoke test: a real sign-up sheet for a free sport + digital taster session, with the fail threshold set before the sheet goes out.

Code all evidence against H1–H3. Segment stakeholders into primary / secondary / tertiary tiers. Write the Problem Validation Report. Founder + working group make the call: go, pivot, or kill.

Decision made with evidence, not conviction. If GO: fundraising opens with primary research in hand — the strongest asset a pre-revenue NGO can carry into a funder meeting.

06 · What we ask

Discovery guide — non-leading by design

GOL, digital skills, and any solution language are banned from weeks 1–4. We ask about lives, not our idea.

Youth
"Walk me through yesterday after school — what did you do, where, with who?"
Maps real free-time behaviour instead of asking hypotheticals.
Youth
"What do you wish existed around here for people your age? What happened to the last thing that came here?"
Surfaces pains and the graveyard of past programmes — the competitive analysis in their words.
Youth
"When you want to learn something new, who do you go to? Where do you look?"
Tests current solutions and whether a skills pathway is a felt job-to-be-done.
Parents
"What worries you most about your kid's time outside school?"
Parents are the permission layer. Their pain defines the primary stakeholder pair.
JPKK / leaders
"Which programmes have come through in the last three years — and why did each one stop?"
The single most valuable question on site. Failure patterns of predecessors are GOL's cheapest lessons.
P

Primary — youth 13–21 & their parents

The beneficiaries. The problem must be theirs, in their words — every hypothesis is scored against this tier only.

S

Secondary — JPKK, schools, coaches, surau

The permission and delivery layer. Interviewed for context and access, not for problem validation.

T

Tertiary — KPKT / DBKL, KBS, funders

Enablers. Deliberately engaged after validation — evidence first, then institutions.

Beneficiary scope is deliberately narrowed for this sprint; broadening again requires evidence.

07 · Honest exits

Kill and pivot criteria — set now, not after

GOL does not proceed as designed if:

  • The problem isn't felt. Fewer than 60% of primary stakeholders articulate the gap unprompted → the problem statement is ours, not theirs. Kill or fundamentally reframe.
  • The space is already served. Existing programmes adequately cover the need → GOL pivots to strengthening incumbents (or exits) rather than duplicating.
  • The hook doesn't transfer. Smoke-test sign-ups fall below the pre-set threshold → the sport → digital link fails, and the core model must be redesigned before any pilot.
  • Access depends on one gatekeeper. If we can only reach residents a JPKK selects for us, the data is captured and the community model is fragile.

Zero-based thinking applies throughout: no line of the previous GOL plan survives into the pilot by default. Everything re-earns its place with field evidence, and success is defined before each stage, not after.

10 · For tomorrow's conversation

Where I'd value your challenge

Five places this plan is most likely to be wrong:

  1. Is 3 sites × ~20 conversations enough signal, or false confidence? Where would you add depth vs breadth?
  2. How do we interview beyond the residents a gatekeeper selects for us — without burning the gatekeeper relationship?
  3. Are student researchers credible in a PPR context, or do we need a community insider embedded in the team?
  4. Is the honorarium clean, or does paying interviewees/volunteers distort what people tell us?
  5. What's the right smoke-test fail threshold for the taster sign-up — and what have you seen work at this stage?
