For Mark to Know

GTO Is Buried — Here’s Why

🚫 GTO is not a negotiating chip anymore. Do not reintroduce it tomorrow.
Wilson has rejected it clearly, consistently, and repeatedly across multiple conversations. Bringing it back will signal to their GM that PSC has not been listening — and will immediately damage the tone of the meeting before the real conversation even starts.

Why Wilson Won’t Accept GTO on 370 sqft

  • ✗Standard retail lease convention: pay base rent or GTO (whichever higher) — not both. Wilson’s retail teams operate by this norm globally.
  • ✗At 370 sqft, a GTO % model requires strong volume to generate meaningful return for PSC — Wilson knows this and won’t take on that obligation for a small-format space.
  • ✗Their GM sees it as a risk-on commitment with no ceiling — especially for a first-time Malaysia partnership with no track record at this location.
  • ✗Ching Huey has said it multiple times. Amy said it. The GM will say it again tomorrow. It is a firm wall.

What GTO Is Being Replaced With

The GTO question is now entirely reframed as: “What does the RM300K marketing package look like, and how much of it is cash?”

Cash in the package is the functional substitute for GTO — it is guaranteed, not performance-linked, and gives PSC the same certainty that a percentage of sales would have provided. The difference: PSC gets paid regardless of how much product Wilson moves.

The reframe for Mark: Stop thinking about GTO. Start thinking about guaranteed cash commitment. A RM100K–150K cash component from Wilson in Year 1 is worth more to PSC than a 6% GTO on an unproven 370-sqft shop — because it’s certain.

Critical Risk

The In-Kind Problem

Wilson will almost certainly open with RM300K as full product-in-kind. Even with a “sell and convert to cash” arrangement, this is not a position PSC should accept. Here’s why — and what the alternative looks like.

If Wilson pays purely in-kind
  • ✗PSC has zero cash to fund activations, events, operations — we have to sell Wilson gear to fund Wilson’s own partnership
  • ✗Sell-through risk sits entirely with PSC — slow-moving inventory means the RM300K never fully materialises
  • ✗Wilson gets brand visibility + a retail outlet with zero cash commitment — PSC carries all the operational weight
  • ✗This is a consignment arrangement dressed in partnership language — not a strategic partnership
  • ✗No cash = no signal that Wilson genuinely values PSC as a partner vs. a distribution point
If Wilson commits cash + structured product
  • ✓Cash funds real activations: coaching clinics, tournaments, crew uniforms, marketing
  • ✓Product-in-kind covers natural use cases: court equipment, demo gear, coach seeding, event prizes
  • ✓Wilson’s financial commitment signals genuine long-term intent — not a retail play
  • ✓PSC can hold Wilson accountable to a proper activation calendar with agreed deliverables and timelines
  • ✓Product drawn down against specific activations — not an open-ended inventory dump that PSC has to manage
The line to use if Wilson opens with full in-kind:
“Cash is how we know you’re a partner, not a supplier. We’re giving Wilson a ready, activated, proven niche community — 24,000 monthly footfall at KLGCC, a T20 demographic, and a platform that costs years to build. That warrants a cash commitment, not just product stock we have to move on your behalf.”
