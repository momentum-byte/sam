The Sol Club

Where TRX connects.

Every day, something worth showing up for. Sport, training, recovery and everything after.

The opportunity

TRX has the buildings. It doesn't have the club.

Twenty thousand people work inside Malaysia's international financial district today, heading toward thirty thousand. Twelve thousand will live here. They have a world-class mall, a ten-acre park, and the city's only MRT interchange under their feet.

What they don't have is somewhere to play, train, recover, and stay — without leaving the precinct.

The proposition

One address. Five reasons to come.

Most racquet venues sell court hours. Sol Club sells a day — and the day is what makes members stay, spend, and come back tomorrow.

1

Sport

3 padel courts and 6 pickleball courts. Competitive standard, social atmosphere. The reason people walk in the first time.

2

Fitness

HYROX-format training zone. The fastest-growing fitness race format in the world, and nothing like it in the KL city centre. Fills weekday mornings and lunch hours that courts alone can't.

3

Wellness

Yoga and pilates studio. Broadens the member base well beyond racquet players — and gives the club a 7am and a 7pm that isn't dependent on court supply.

4

Recovery

Ice plunge and hot tub. This is the ritual that turns a session into a habit. It's also the single most photographed thing in every club that has one.

5

Social

Food and drink built for after — not an afterthought. Where the community actually forms. The courts start the relationship; the bar keeps it.

Under consideration: golf simulator bays indoors, mini putt on the outdoor terrace. Both are strong corporate-event and off-peak revenue plays. Decision point in Q4 once the space plan is fixed.
Why it wins

The moat isn't the sport. It's the address and the community.

Nobody else is inside the district

Every competitor asks a TRX professional to drive fifteen to thirty minutes. We ask them to take a lift.

Corporate demand is walk-up demand

Ninety per cent of TRX tenants are international corporates with wellness budgets. Their people are already here at 8am.

Five reasons beats one reason

A courts business dies at 10am on a Tuesday. A club with training, wellness, recovery and F&B doesn't.

Community is the retention engine

Leagues, academies and club collaborations are what make a venue hard to leave. That's the part competitors can't copy by building courts.

Tech

One system, not five

The member experience is the product. A member should book a court, join a class, tap into recovery, and settle a bar tab on one account with one balance. That only works if it's designed as a single stack from the start — retrofitting it later is expensive and members feel the seams.

Booking and CRM

Courts, classes, coaching, recovery slots. Corporate account hierarchy so a company admin manages their own block.

Integrated POS

F&B, pro shop, events, court walk-ins — one ledger, one member profile, one view of spend.

Credits wallet

Members buy credits annually or monthly. Credits spend across everything. Roll-over, top-up, auto-renew. This is what turns transactional play into recurring revenue.

Access control

Tap-in at the door and at the recovery zone. Ties utilisation data back to the member record.

Member app

Book, pay, see the ladder, join a league, bring a guest. The club in a pocket.

Why it matters commercially: a credits wallet converts pay-per-play into annual recurring revenue, and it lets you sell to a corporate before a single court is poured. That's the mechanism that makes pre-sales real rather than aspirational.
Why it matters operationally: one system is also how a lean team runs a seventeen-hour day. Every process the software doesn't handle becomes a person we have to hire.
Community

A club is people, not courts.

You can copy a facility. You can't copy a community. Sol Club opens with a programme calendar already running and partnerships already signed — so the club has a pulse from day one rather than waiting to develop one.

Club collaborations

Open doors, not closed gates

The KL racquet scene has real communities already — leagues, social groups, competitive circles. A premium club that shuts them out gets an empty midweek. A club that hosts them gets a full one, plus a conversion pipeline into membership.

Host, don't compete

Existing pickleball and padel communities get preferential off-peak blocks. They fill dead hours, bring their own energy, and a percentage convert to members.

Inter-club leagues

Sol Club as the anchor venue for a KL-wide club league. Home fixtures bring rival clubs and their supporters through our doors — and our brand into theirs.

Every level, deliberately

Beginner socials, intermediate ladders, competitive squads. Premium doesn't have to mean intimidating. The beginner who walks in nervous at 34 becomes the member who's still here at 40.

Corporate leagues

TRX tenant versus TRX tenant. HSBC against Accenture on a Thursday evening. This writes itself, and it's the single best corporate membership sales tool we'll have.

Guest instructors and residencies

Independent yoga, pilates and strength coaches run named sessions under our roof. They bring their following, we supply the room and the audience. Low fixed cost, high variety.

Academy

In-house coaching, every level

Coaching in-house rather than outsourced — because quality control, member relationships, and margin all matter, and because a coach who works for the club builds the club.

Pathways

Padel and pickleball academies

Structured progression, not ad-hoc lessons. A member knows what level they're at and what's next. Retention follows progression.

HYROX training programmes

Small-group coached sessions on a race format that has an actual finish line. Goal-driven fitness retains far better than open gym floor.

Junior development

Weekend and school-holiday programmes. Juniors bring parents, and parents buy memberships. TRX residents are the immediate catchment.

Coach development

We train and certify our own. Consistent standard across every court, and a bench that scales when we add capacity.

Team

Lean by design, not by accident.

Revenue starts when the doors open. Every hire before that is pure cost, so the sequence matters more than the headcount. My view: hire four people who can each build their own function, give them ownership of their own teams, and keep everything else variable.

Four core hires. Everything below them is part-time, outsourced, or hired by the head who owns it — with me in the final interview.

The logic

Why lean, and where I'd spend

Four heads, not eight managers

Fewer, better, more expensive people beats a broad org chart every time in a pre-opening. Each of these four should be able to run their function without me in the room.

Front of house is variable, not fixed

A seventeen-hour day with peaks at 7am, 1pm and 6pm is a rostering problem, not a headcount problem. Part-time shifts flex with demand. One Duty Manager holds the standard.

Coaches earn from what they deliver

Two salaried head coaches for standards and pathways. Everyone else per-session. Academy margin scales with demand instead of sitting as fixed cost through a slow month.

F&B is a partner, not a payroll

A subtenant operator brings their own team and their own P&L. We take a revenue share and hold the brand standard. Running a kitchen ourselves would add a dozen heads and a whole category of risk we don't need.

Finance stays outsourced until it can't

Bookkeeping and payroll go to a firm. A controller comes in-house in Feb 2027 only if transaction volume genuinely justifies it.

Where I would not economise

The four core hires and the tech stack. Underpaying a head of growth to save RM60k a year is how you miss a RM2M pipeline. Cheap software is how you end up hiring three people to work around it.

For discussion

What I'd like the board's view on

Is the sequence right?

I've put Business Growth first because revenue has to exist before the building does. An argument exists for Ops and Tech first, since the systems have a longer lead time. I'd like to hear the case.

Which names do you already have?

Mark and Martin will both have people in mind. I'd rather blend networks than run parallel searches — bring the names and let's put them against these four roles.

Is four too lean?

My view is no — but I'm carrying GM plus a fifth function myself until Q1 2027, and the board should know that's the trade I'm making to keep OPEX down.

Hiring authority

I'd want final call on all four core hires and everything below them. Board sign-off on the compensation envelope, not on the individuals. That's the practical line that lets this move at pace.
